# Spanish national election archive

128 records: 71 historical and modern lower-house elections (1810–2023), 40 elected Senate contests (1871–2023), nine European elections (1987–2024), two restricted Franco-era family-representative contests, the parliamentary election of Amadeo I, two Republican presidential ballots and the separately linked 1936 citizen-elector election. Two existing confirmed 29 November 2026 Calendar records are retained under their original IDs, linked as Congress and Senate, with 350 and 209 elected seats respectively (the 2026 constitutional reform gives Formentera a separate senator).

Congress's official chronology establishes the historical lower-house contests, including dates and early year-only records. Electoral decrees take precedence over conflicting 1896 and 1903 date labels. Senate polling days are distinct; four nineteenth-century Senate electoral-college dates remain unknown instead of copying the Congress day. Restoration senators by right and royal life appointments are excluded. Modern Senate results exclude royal appointments in 1977 and autonomous-community designations. Historic franchise restrictions and unopposed Article 29 seats are explicit. Such source turnout figures concern the contested electorate.

Certified JEC 2023 results take precedence over summaries. Congress has 350 seats and 24,688,087 valid ballots, including 200,673 blank ballots. PSOE and PSC are grouped once. All 1,152 Senate candidate rows are counted once, including 208 elected candidates. Santiago Llorente's later corrected count is 1,007,322; the certified candidate-vote total is 67,766,271. Senate shares concern candidate selections, not people voting: voters may choose multiple candidates. No turnout is derived from those selections.

Certified European 2024 results include the July correction transferring 157 votes from Junts to PSOE: 442,140 and 5,291,102 respectively. The valid-ballot denominator includes a labelled blank row, and all 61 seats reconcile. The 2009 and 2019 European Parliament constitutive-session delegations have 50 and 54 seats, excluding later Lisbon and Brexit reallocations. Their primary published percentages are kept without mixing secondary raw counts on a different denominator.

The 1931 presidential winner has 362 of 410 participating parliamentary votes; the official 1936 Gaceta proclamation gives Azaña 754 of 847 cast, distinct from the 911-member assembly. Only those confirmed winning tallies are entered. The Congress summary of the 1870 monarch election itemizes 309 votes without establishing a complete ballot denominator; percentages remain empty. The 1936 electoral blocs use final May seat totals after later scrutiny/reruns; conflicting detailed party allocations and first-round estimated votes are not mixed into that table. Incomplete historical results remain partial. The 1967 and 1971 restricted elections cover only 102 and 104 family representatives; the appointed/corporate whole chamber's 564-seat totals are not presented as elected party results, and the contests are assessed as uncompetitive.

Scope excludes appointed chambers and royal nomination/selection procedures, local/regional contests, individual by-elections, referendums, government investitures and regent appointments. Prime ministers are not directly elected. Party rankings do not imply government formation.
